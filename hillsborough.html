The catalysing moment, 2009

Burnham became Secretary of State for Culture, Media and Sport in January 2008. As the minister responsible for sport, he was due to speak at Anfield on 15 April 2009, the 20th anniversary of the Hillsborough disaster. The chant of "Justice for the 96" that interrupted his speech that day is generally treated as the catalysing moment of his Hillsborough campaign. The day after, in the Commons Oral Answers to Culture, Media and Sport, Burnham made three substantive contributions on Hillsborough (source, source, source).

He left the Cabinet for Health in June 2009, and Government in May 2010, but the campaign continued. The Coalition Government issued the formal Hillsborough Apology on 28 June 2010 (source). The Hillsborough Independent Panel was confirmed to continue under the new government on 22 July 2010 (source), with a written statement on the same day setting out its terms (source).

The Hillsborough Law speech, March 2017

On 29 March 2017, weeks before leaving Westminster for the GMCA mayoralty, Burnham introduced the Public Authority (Accountability) Bill, more commonly known as the Hillsborough Law. The Ten Minute Rule Bill speech is in the Commons spine at source and was identified in the the archive's PDF extraction pass as a touchstone contribution. The Bill sought a statutory duty of candour for public bodies, equality of legal funding at inquests, and offences for police officers and officials who knowingly mislead.

The Bill itself was not enacted in that Parliament. Its substance has resurfaced repeatedly since, most directly in the Hillsborough Charter agreed in December 2023.

Bishop James Jones's review, November 2017

The Bishop James Jones review, "The Patronising Disposition of Unaccountable Power", was published on 1 November 2017. The gov.uk landing page is at source (gov.uk hosts the publication under the title "Hillsborough Stadium Disaster: lessons that must be learnt"). The accompanying press release announcing publication is at source. The full 122-page review text is now in the archive at source (archive PDF extraction), making the specific review findings and the precursor proposals to the Hillsborough Charter directly citable. The phrase "the patronising disposition of unaccountable power" has since become Burnham's most-cited frame for the entire Hillsborough story.

The Hillsborough Charter and government response, December 2023

It took six years for the government to respond formally to the Bishop Jones review. The response landed on 6 December 2023 as an oral statement, source. The gov.uk landing page for the published Hillsborough Charter is at source; the substantive six-point Charter text was extracted from the image-only PDF via OCR in the archive's PDF extraction pass and is now in the archive at source. The full government response to the Bishop Jones report is at source. A news story two days later set out the government's pledge "never to repeat the injustices of Hillsborough" (source).

The Charter itself is short and substantive: six commitments by HM Government to learn the lessons of Hillsborough, signed on the Government's behalf by Oliver Dowden as Deputy Prime Minister. The commitments cover emergency response, public-interest priority over reputation, candour in scrutiny processes, refusal to defend the indefensible, mutual respect with the public, and accountability and openness to challenge. The full Charter text is in source.

A further commitment to the Charter was made on 18 March 2025, at source.

The continuing campaign

Throughout the GMCA mayoralty period, Burnham has continued to call for the full Hillsborough Law in its original 2017 form rather than the more limited statutory duty of candour ultimately adopted. The GMCA layer of the archive carries multiple records of his interventions on this and on related campaigns (Orgreave, contaminated blood, the Hillsborough families' experience of inquests). The contaminated blood petitioning week of 25 April 2017 is preserved in the petition-family records raw/commons/2017/2017-04-25_*_contaminated-blood.md (16 records), Burnham's final week as MP for Leigh before taking office as Mayor of Greater Manchester.
