Andrew Murray Burnham was born in Liverpool in 1970 and raised near Warrington, the son of a telephone engineer and a receptionist. Before Parliament he worked as a researcher for Tessa Jowell, at the NHS Confederation, as administrator of the Football Task Force and as a special adviser to Culture Secretary Chris Smith. The archive's own record begins with his election for Leigh in June 2001; the chapters below follow it from there. Use the era chips on the search page to confine any search to a single chapter.

June 2001 – May 2005

Backbencher, MP for Leigh

Elected at 31. The early record is constituency-minded and health-focused: a seat on the Health Select Committee, a maiden speech tying Europe to jobs in Leigh, and loyal lobbies through the Iraq divisions of 2003, which he has revisited publicly ever since. PPS to David Blunkett from 2003.

May 2005 – June 2009

Minister: Home Office, Health, Treasury, Culture

Four posts in four years: Under-Secretary at the Home Office, Minister of State for Delivery at Health, Chief Secretary to the Treasury at 37, then Secretary of State for Culture, Media and Sport from January 2008. It was as Culture Secretary, at Anfield on the 20th anniversary in April 2009, that the "Justice for the 96" chant interrupted his speech and set the course of his later politics.

June 2009 – May 2010

Secretary of State for Health

Eleven months in Cabinet under Gordon Brown: the Mid Staffordshire statement and apology, the swine flu response, and the National Care Service white paper, the policy he has carried in different forms through every role since. Read the full Health Secretary briefing.

  • 24 Feb 2010Mid Staffordshire oral statement: the apology to the families

May 2010 – May 2015

Opposition: leadership candidate and Shadow Health Secretary

Fourth in the 2010 leadership contest won by Ed Miliband, then the long shadow-health war against the Lansley reforms, fought under the banner of repealing the Health and Social Care Act. The Hillsborough campaign reached its parliamentary turning points in these years: the Independent Panel report of September 2012 and the quashed inquests. Read the Hillsborough briefing.

  • 12 Sep 2012PMQs on the day of the Hillsborough Independent Panel report
  • 2010–2015The shadow-health record
May 2015 – May 2017

Second leadership bid, Shadow Home Secretary, the road out

Runner-up to Jeremy Corbyn in 2015, then Shadow Home Secretary through the EU referendum. His last major act as an MP was introducing the Hillsborough Law, weeks before leaving Westminster: the Public Authority (Accountability) Bill of March 2017. He fought and won the first Greater Manchester mayoral election on a manifesto written from Leigh.

May 2017 – June 2026

Mayor of Greater Manchester

Three terms and three growing mandates. The defining delivery was the Bee Network: the first city-region outside London to take its buses back under public control, phase by phase from 2023. The defining persona was "King of the North", built in the October 2020 stand-off with the government over Tier 3 support. Read the Bee Network briefing.

June 2026 – present

Labour leader and Prime Minister

After Keir Starmer's resignation he entered the leadership race from outside Parliament, was declared leader unopposed on 17 July 2026 with nominations from over ninety per cent of Labour MPs, and became Prime Minister on 20 July. The opening weeks set the pattern the archive now tracks daily: cost of living first (VAT off electricity bills, the £2 bus cap), the social care commitment renewed, the early-release prisons row, and the drought summer.
